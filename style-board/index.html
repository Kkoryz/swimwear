    We start by matching recycled fabrics to the stretch, hand feel, print and colour your swimwear needs. Then we source options that suit your design and budget.

    • Regenerated Fibre Performance

      Waste such as plastic items and old carpets is cleaned, sorted, liquefied and reformed, then made into fabric. Regenerated fibre performs much like new fibre. Any difference in performance comes from the fibre type and the way the fabric is constructed, not from the fibre being recycled.

    • Sustainable Fabric Choices

      On request, we source ECONYL regenerated nylon, including Italian Carvico ECONYL fabrics, REPREVE recycled polyester and 84/16 recycled polyester-spandex. We also offer organic and other sustainable fabrics, and we source these only from certified material suppliers.

    • Requested Fabrics And Trade-offs

      If you ask, we source imported GRS- or OEKO-TEX-certified recycled fabrics, regenerated Lycra, and waste-regenerated fabrics coloured with OEKO-TEX-certified dyes. Branded or imported certified options cost more and come in fewer colours with less stock, so a tight budget or deadline narrows your choice.

    • Swimwear Fabric Construction

      We make your swimwear in nylon or polyester blended with spandex, such as 80% nylon 20% spandex, with matching linings. Chlorine-resistant LYCRA Xtra Life is available if you ask for it.

    • Production And Waste

      We use water-saving, low-carbon production processes, manage production waste strictly and follow carbon-neutral packaging strategies. Choosing recycled polyester helps your line cut plastic waste.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Can you show examples of swimwear you've made for brands selling in Europe or North America?

    Yes. Every year we make samples and bulk orders for more than 350 brands, and some of those labels sell in Europe and North America. Here are three recent projects, with client names kept private. A start-up bikini label in the United States came to us with sketches and a mood board for its first collection. Our in-house design team turned them into patterns graded from XS to XL, and the fit samples were ready in 10 to 15 days. The label launched six styles at our 50-piece minimum per style in each colour, shipped them DDP to its warehouse and reordered its two best sellers the next season. A swim school in Canada needed chlorine-resistant training suits in its club colours, with its logo printed and sizes from children's to adult. We matched the club colours to its Pantone references, sublimated the logo on chlorine-resistant polyester and packed mixed sizes in each carton at no extra cost. Bulk took 35 to 40 days after sample approval, and the approved style now repeats without a new sample. A plus-size swimwear label in Germany moved a range to us after cups gaped and straps dug in on its previous factory's run. We redrafted the styles on a 2XL base, wet-tested the samples on a 2XL fit model and graded them up to 5XL. An SGS inspector checked the bulk before shipment, and the label relaunched the range the following season. For those markets, our production is certified to OEKO-TEX Standard 100, GRS, RCS, bluesign, ISO 9001, WRAP and BSCI. We ship on FOB, CIF or DDP terms. If your market requires test reports for the styles you order, we have those styles tested at an accredited lab, and the lab fees go on your balance invoice at cost.

    Have you ever taken over a production that went wrong at another factory, and what did you do?

    Yes. When we take over a run that went wrong at another factory, we find the fault and get a corrected sample approved before bulk production starts again. Most failed runs come from mistakes in the pattern, grading or fit, not from the sewing. If we produce again from the same faulty pattern, the defect comes back at our factory too. Switching factories doesn't fix it on its own. Send us your approved sample or tech pack along with a few of the faulty pieces, and we'll reply within one business day. After we agree on the price, our in-house design team fixes the pattern, grading and fit and makes a new sample in 10 to 15 days. Bulk production then takes 35 to 40 days from when you approve the sample and we receive your deposit, depending on order size and season. The extra sample round means delivery takes longer than just re-running the order. If the original fault was clearly in the fabric or trims and not the fit, that extra round adds little. We also accept fabric and trims you've already bought for the order. We inspect them when they arrive and tell you about any shortages or defects within 3 business days, before cutting.

    Have you worked with small start-up brands placing their first order, like us?

    Yes. We work with new labels on their first order, and we offer a starter pack and separate startup packages for your first collection. The starter pack contains three finished samples, twenty fabric swatches, a catalogue of the ready-made styles we keep in stock and a short guide to launching your own swimwear brand. The startup packages cover design, development and manufacturing. Many new labels combine these packages with our consulting service. The starter pack and the startup packages are offered separately.

    Can I speak to one of the brands you've worked with as a reference?

    Yes, but only once that client has given written consent. When they do, we put you in touch within 5 business days of your request. We keep our private-label clients anonymous, and that includes brands whose failed production runs we've taken over. So we never share a reference contact without permission. In the meantime, we can send you our catalogue and up to three garments from earlier production runs within 1 to 2 business days, depending on availability.

    Rash Guard Fabrics, UPF Ratings and Certifications

    Through Swimwear Fabrics & Sourcing, we source 80/20 nylon-spandex with matching linings, and we supply AS/NZS 4399 UPF 50+ documentation; extra lab testing is charged at cost.

    Performance fabric range

    Our four-way-stretch, UV-protective polyester-spandex weighs 180gsm to 320gsm. Water temperature and activity decide the weight first. Board contact and sun exposure decide the fibre.

    Lightweight warm-water fabrics

    At 180 to 200gsm, these fabrics hold little heat and air-dry in about 30 minutes. They suit warm-water surfing, swim lessons and long days in the sun.

    Heavier supportive fabrics

    At 260 to 320gsm, these fabrics keep wearers warmer in cooler water and give firmer compression and muscle support for training and watersports. The trade-off is that they take about 60 minutes to dry.

    Repeated board contact

    For repeated board contact, we recommend tightly knitted 200–240 GSM polyester-spandex or 80/20 nylon-spandex. Nylon stands up to rubbing best, and polyester holds its colour better in sun and salt water.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How do you attach embroidered patches to stretch swim fabric, and can I order the patches by themselves?

    We sew patches on around the edge with a zigzag stitch or coverstitch, so the seam stretches with your suit. We don't use heat-seal glue backings because they lift in chlorine and salt water. Yes, you can order patches on their own, starting at 50 pieces per design.

    What is the smallest lettering, the largest embroidery size and the most thread colours you can stitch in one design on swimwear?

    We can stitch lettering as small as 5 mm high with lines at least 1 mm thick. For 3D puff, the minimum is 10 mm high. One hooping fits a design up to 30 x 30 cm, and each design can use up to 12 thread colours.

    Can you embroider on top of a printed swimsuit fabric or combine embroidery with sequins and appliqué in one design?

    Yes. We combine embroidery with sequins, appliqué, printing, laser cutting or embossing. Once your sublimation or digital print is set, we stitch over it and match the thread to your print colours. Beads and sequins go on after the embroidery, away from seams and straps, so they don't rub or snag.

    What drives the embroidery cost on my order: stitch count, colours, size, technique or number of logo positions?

    Stitch count affects cost the most, and fill density drives stitch count more than size does. A solid fill costs more than an outline of the same size. Each logo position needs its own hooping and machine run. Extra colours, 3D puff, appliqué and patches add setup and handling time, and digitising adds a one-off setup charge per design. If you choose outline or open-fill designs with fewer positions and colours, you keep your cost down and the suit stays comfortable.

    Can you scale the same embroidered logo across children's and adult sizes while keeping its lettering and detail readable?

    Yes. We make 2 to 3 size versions, for example about 4 cm wide for children's sizes and 7 cm wide for adult sizes. We redigitise each version instead of shrinking the file, so the lettering stays at least 5 mm high and fine detail stays readable.

    Can you embroider fabric or pre-cut swimwear panels supplied by us, rather than only your own production or finished garments?

    Yes. We embroider fabric or pre-cut swimwear panels that you supply, from 50 pieces per design. 3% spare panels are added to cover setup and machine loss.

    Which appliqué fabrics do you offer that remain compatible with the base fabric's stretch and wet-use requirements?

    We use a stretch swim fabric similar to your base fabric, so both layers stretch and dry together. The options are 80% nylon 20% spandex and polyester spandex, and on request chlorine-resistant LYCRA Xtra Life, recycled ECONYL nylon and REPREVE polyester. We also offer metallic foil spandex, swim-grade sequin fabric and stretch mesh. We edge them with a stretch satin stitch and back them with a soft facing for wet use.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    For your digital printing, do the number of artwork colours or the amount of printed coverage change the production cost?

    The number of colours doesn't change your digital printing cost, because every design prints in one pass at the same rate. Coverage does matter when you compare print types. We cost all-over prints by the metre of fabric, and a small placement print on a plain piece costs less than printing the whole garment.

    Can you print on a black or coloured base fabric, or does my print have to go on white fabric?

    We print on black or coloured bases, but sublimation only produces tones darker than the base. All-over sublimation needs a polyester-rich fabric. Its dyes are transparent, so they give the truest colours on white or light bases and can't print lighter than the fabric. For white or pastel details, we use a white base, or digital pigment printing with a white underlayer on dark fabric.

    Can I order custom-printed swimwear fabric from you without ordering garment manufacturing?

    No. We only offer custom printing on swimwear we manufacture for you, and we don't take fabric-only orders. Any printed fabric we produce ships with your garment order.
