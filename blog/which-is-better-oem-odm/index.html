Which is better, OEM or ODM?

By Wenjie DengPublished 17 November 20268 min read

For a US swimwear brand, OEM is usually better when distinctive fit or construction is essential and you can support custom development. ODM is usually better when an existing design meets your brief and you want to reduce development work. Compare the actual samples, launch cash, delivery plan, and file handover before choosing; the manufacturing label alone does not settle those questions. The general tradeoff is explained in SEACOMP's OEM vs ODM manufacturing guide.

At Strandmill, we work from your design, a reference picture, or an idea, and we also offer ready designs. Your starting point should be the product promise: what must a customer feel, see, or experience that a standard style cannot already deliver?

Choosing OEM or ODM for your swimwear launch

Choose OEM if the fit or construction you need goes beyond what a ready style can demonstrate. Choose ODM if a tested existing style meets your brief and the quoted cash requirement and schedule work for you. Neither route automatically means lower minimums, a cheaper delivered order, or production files you can take elsewhere.

Start with a physical sample alongside your design brief. Check the silhouette, coverage, lining, and support before deciding how much development your design needs.

Here, OEM means making a buyer-defined design, while ODM means manufacturer-led design, often based on an existing style. OEM gives you more room to develop something distinctive; an existing ODM design can cut development spending and time. Brightpearl's OEM vs ODM guide explains the distinction. Suppliers use these terms differently, so ask who develops the pattern, who approves changes, and exactly what the proposal covers.

Compare launch cash on the same basis

Ask for each proposal to use the same fabric, lining, size mix, quality requirements, packaging, and US delivery address. Keep garment prices separate from development, sample courier charges, freight, and import costs. A smaller sample bill only helps if the rest of the order still works within your budget.

Our starting minimum is 50 pieces per style in each color, spread across 3 to 4 sizes. Your fabric and print choices can change the exact minimum. A bikini top and matching bottom count as one piece when you order them as a set; tops and bottoms sold separately count separately. Put that unit definition into your comparison before multiplying the quantities.

We charge USD 50 per design, covering its pattern or print across colorways, along with the tech pack and CAD pattern we prepare. Each physical sample costs USD 50, and each revised fit sample is charged as a new sample. Sample courier delivery costs USD 50. These fees are not credited against bulk. Minor digital adjustments are free, and we remake a sample without charge if it differs significantly from the approved handover. Our Development page can help you prepare the sampling scope.

For this worked budget, assume a solid-color swimsuit with the same nylon-spandex fabric and lining, the same S/M/L size mix, and standard packing. There is no new print, hardware, or separate lab testing. Assume 100 garments at USD 18 each for OEM and 100 garments at USD 19 each for ODM. These garment prices and logistics allowances are assumed inputs, not Strandmill quotations. Both routes include a charged design. OEM needs an initial sample and a revised fit sample, shipped separately; ODM needs only an initial sample and courier shipment.

In this example, you spend USD 250 on samples and design for OEM, compared with USD 150 for ODM. This covers your inventory, not your whole business launch. Keep photography, store, advertising, storage, and your returns reserve outside this budget, then include them in your business cash plan. When calculating the total order cost, do not count the deposit twice.

If the assumed garment prices and logistics allowances stay the same, an order of 50 garments brings the OEM total to USD 1,700 and the ODM total to USD 1,650. At 200 garments, the totals become USD 4,400 for OEM and USD 4,500 for ODM. This calculation shows how development spending can have more influence at smaller quantities, while a lower garment price can have more influence at larger quantities.

Get fresh prices for each quantity rather than treating these assumptions as a volume quote. Ask for minimums by style, color, and size allocation, plus separate accessory minimums where they apply. For a delivered proposal, check which freight, duty, and clearance costs are included, and keep the supporting breakdown. Our DDP option covers delivery to your door, and we handle duties, customs, and freight through our forwarding partners, so do not add those charges again. You can find order and shipping questions in our FAQ.

Find the boundary of a ready style's fit

Approve print changes separately from fit changes. Keep a change log that records the request, its effect on the pattern, the sample outcome, and final approval. JLCCNC's OEM vs ODM comparison explains the general principle that modifications depend on the existing platform, but your swimwear decisions still need evidence from the garment.

In our anonymized long-torso swimsuit review, the buyer's logo placement worked on a ready style without changing the pattern. A firmer lining changed how the leg opening felt, while the requested torso length and fuller front coverage needed a revised body pattern and regrading. The buyer chose custom development because the ready silhouette did not provide the intended fit.

Look inside the garment as well as at its outside shape. On a supported swimsuit, you can compare visible details such as the lining attachment and underbust elastic before requesting a different level of support.

The pattern-maker's final change log for that review recorded the outcomes below. The measurements are for the finished garment laid flat, using the buyer's agreed front-body measurement from shoulder seam to crotch seam.

These results apply to that particular style, fabric, and revision. For your range, record the wearer's fit comments alongside the measurements, and repeat the check wherever coverage or support changes between sizes. A photograph can show you the lining and seam placement, but it cannot establish stretch recovery or support on its own.

We fit the base size to your measurement sheet and wear-test it wet for stretch, coverage, and opacity before grading the range. For light colors, we check wet opacity again on the finished garment under stretch before bulk approval. Our fabric and trim reports cover the materials actually tested. We can arrange order-specific laboratory reports at your cost. Use our One Piece Swimsuit Manufacturer page to define your product brief and our Custom Swimwear Printing page for your artwork needs.

Plan backward from US delivery

An existing design can shorten development, but the time saved depends on fabric availability and how quickly approvals happen. SEACOMP's manufacturing comparison explains this general speed advantage. To make a useful booking decision, you need a project schedule that also accounts for your waiting time and delivery.

Our standard sample takes 10 to 15 days. Bulk production usually takes 35 to 40 days after sample approval and receipt of your deposit, depending on quantity and season. Allow an extra 2 to 3 weeks for a new mold or print. These production windows do not cover the full journey to your US receiving address.

Assign an owner and date to each launch milestone

Start with the date your warehouse needs to receive stock and work backward. Reserve receiving time, then allow for freight and clearance, inspection, bulk production, and approvals. Check that the fabric is physically available and the production slot is secured. A ready style can lose its expected head start while waiting for special fabric. If you request a fit change after approval, get a revised schedule before committing to your launch date.

Check what you can take to another factory

Ask separately about ownership, access, and usable file formats. inFlow's OEM vs ODM guide explains the difference between existing background IP and newly developed foreground IP. Rather than relying on the word “custom,” ask for an inventory of what you will actually receive, including the approved revision.

Our ready-to-go styles stay available to other buyers, but we use the prints, artwork, and branding you add only for you. Custom and ODM designs developed for your brand are exclusive to you. For fully custom projects, the tech packs, CAD patterns, and digitized files we deliver become yours once you pay the related fees. We keep copies for your reorders and do not use them for other clients.

Our redacted custom handover inventory listed the approved tech pack as PDF, the final pattern as DXF, a graded nest as DXF, and the measurement sheet as XLSX. The release record confirmed paid development fees and permission to produce at another factory, while excluding the unrelated ready-style library. That project had no separate file-transfer charge. Factory changes included in the delivered custom pattern went with the pattern; general sewing methods stayed outside the asset package.

Have the receiving factory open the files and check the size range, seam allowances, and revision against the approved garment. Exclusivity tells you who may use the design; a file inventory tells you what another factory can work with. For your own project, confirm the territory, duration, permitted reuse, release conditions, and any transfer charge in writing. Compare OEM and ODM against one swimwear brief

Use the same collection brief for both proposals. Include your intended customer, fit and support needs, fabric and lining, size range, style and color quantities, cash budget, and required US delivery date. Attach reference garments or a measurement sheet so the sample team can evaluate what “better fit” means for you.

Ask for sample evidence, a dated milestone plan, a delivered-cost breakdown, and written file-handover terms with each proposal. Start the comparison through our Request a Quote page. Choose the route that meets your brief with approved samples and a delivery plan you can afford.
