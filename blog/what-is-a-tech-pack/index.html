What Is a Tech Pack?

By Wenjie DengPublished 17 November 202611 min read

A tech pack, short for technical package, is the instruction document a clothing manufacturer uses to develop and make a garment. It brings together drawings, materials, measurements, construction details and revisions so the brand and factory can work from the same specifications. See UpHance's guide to creating a fashion tech pack.

For a swimwear brand, the practical question is whether those instructions describe the suit you want well enough to quote, sample and approve. A beautiful sketch cannot settle how a lining behaves when wet or where a leg opening should sit. Those decisions need technical development and a physical sample.

What a tech pack tells your clothing manufacturer

A tech pack tells your manufacturer what to make and how to check it. It helps with quoting and sampling, but the measurement sheet inside it doesn't replace the separate sewing pattern; Techpacker's explanation of a tech pack covers this well.

Picture a scoop-back swimsuit with a lined body and elasticated openings. Its silhouette goes in the drawings, its component choices in the materials list, and its finished dimensions in the measurement sheet. The document map below keeps those jobs apart.

DeliverableWhat it tells youWhat it cannot establish alone
Design briefIntended customer, coverage, look and useExact construction
Full tech packCoordinated instructions and approval historyPhysical fit
Measurement spec sheetWhere and how to measure the finished garmentComplete materials and assembly
Sewing patternShapes used to cut the garment componentsWhether the finished suit feels right
Sewn sampleHow the specified combination performsInstructions for future production unless recorded

At Strandmill, you can start with sketches, reference images or a garment. You decide the intended coverage, customer, color, size range and quantity, and our team works out the patterns, sizing and construction with you. We quote before sampling, and you approve the sample before bulk production goes ahead with the deposit. If you'd like help turning a reference into specifications, have a look at our Development page.

Our scoop-back development case started with a founder's front and back references, a request for fuller seat coverage, and a solid navy color. The handover left out the lining edge treatment, so on the sample the lining rolled into view at the leg opening. We corrected the construction callout and lining pattern, then checked the replacement sample wet and dry. That was a specification omission, and it's separate from the buyer's later request to adjust coverage.

Treat quotation assumptions as questions you need to close: Is the lining full-body? Are the straps adjustable? Is the logo printed or sewn on? A proposed answer can support an estimate, but the sampling handover needs an instruction you've both agreed.

On fully custom projects, the tech packs and CAD patterns we deliver are yours once the related fees are paid. Agree the editable formats and file inventory at the start. Owning a custom pattern doesn't mean the measurement table itself can be used for cutting.

What should a tech pack include?

A tech pack should hold garment drawings, a bill of materials, measurements, construction instructions, color and artwork references, labels, packaging and revisions. You'll find these core contents in Shopify's guide to creating a tech pack. Swimwear also needs clear instructions for the shell, lining, elastic and fit.

Use this readiness worksheet to tell provisional quote information apart from approved production information. Give each open field to a person instead of leaving a blank nobody owns.

Body measurements describe the wearer, while finished-garment measurements describe the suit. Beside each point of measure, write the unit, the endpoints, whether it's relaxed or stretched, and the adjustable-strap setting. A stretched measurement also needs an agreed method so that different reviewers don't pull with different force.

Our long-torso review gives you a finished-garment example: front-body length was measured flat from the shoulder seam to the crotch seam. These values belong to that style, not to every swimsuit.

SizeTarget front-body lengthFinal sampleAcceptance
S67 cm67.4 cmWithin agreed body tolerance
M69 cm69.3 cmWithin agreed body tolerance
L71 cm71.5 cmWithin agreed body tolerance

Our body-measurement tolerance is ±1 cm, and straps, bindings and measurements under 10 cm have a ±0.5 cm tolerance. The target progression above applies only to that reviewed style. A fit review still has to check coverage, movement and wet behavior, because passing the measurement check doesn't automatically mean the fit is approved.

For that scoop-back style, we used SHELL-NAVY, LINING-SOFT and ELASTIC-LEG as component references. The inside drawing tied LINING-SOFT to a turned, secured leg edge and ELASTIC-LEG to the enclosed opening. A firmer lining trial pulled against the shell during movement, so the approved combination kept the softer lining and the revised edge construction.

Keep physical swatches with the component references. If you need help choosing the shell and a matching lining, see our Swimwear Fabrics & Sourcing page.

Round off the handover inventory with the approved PDF, editable source, artwork, base pattern, graded pattern files and physical reference. Our redacted custom handover included a PDF pack, a DXF pattern and graded nest, and an XLSX measurement sheet. Give creation to the technical or artwork owner, approval to the brand, and delivery to the named project contact. Then confirm the actual formats for your project.

How do I make my own tech pack?

Begin with your style brief. Then add annotated drawings, a materials list, measurement and construction pages, color or artwork instructions, and a revision log. Flag any choices that are still open for technical review, and update the pack once you've sampled. Techpacker's What Is a Tech Pack? The Complete Fashion Guide lays out the basic sequence.

You'll move through five stages: Brief, Specify, Sample, Review and Approve. Your first job is to explain who the customer is, how much coverage you want and what the silhouette looks like. By the time the pack is finished, those choices need to be instructions the sample team can follow.

Move from decisions to an approved specification

In our scoop-back pack, the founder's fuller-seat reference became a marked back outline. The inside drawing tied LINING-SOFT to the lining panel and ELASTIC-LEG to the enclosed opening, with SHELL-NAVY on the exterior. Every component reference matched a swatch and a supplier entry in the materials table. When the technician asked, "How is the lining edge secured?", the answer became a construction decision instead of a comment nobody answered.

Set up that layout in an editable document with linked materials and measurement tables. Next to each measurement, enter the unit, the endpoints, whether it's relaxed or stretched, the target and the tolerance. Keep the intended body fit separate from the finished-garment dimensions. Make sure artwork placement and color references match across the drawings and attachments.

For our scoop-back material trial, we cut strips across the fabric and marked a 10 cm span. Each strip held a 500 g load for 30 seconds, then rested unloaded for 60 seconds. Dry, the shell stretched to 15 cm and the soft lining to 14 cm, and both came back to 10.1 cm. After a 10-minute soak in water, they stretched to 15.5 cm and 14.5 cm and came back to 10.2 cm. These were workshop comparisons for this material combination only.

Next, our patternmaker compared elastic cut lengths of 27 cm and 28 cm, each with the same 1 cm overlap, on the same leg-opening pattern with identical stitching. The shorter one pinched during movement, and the longer one stayed in place. The approved entry kept the 30 cm relaxed opening target and specified the longer elastic with the secured lining edge. Wet fit photos backed up that choice. The strip extension results alone didn't set the garment dimensions.

In the sample correction log, we changed the lining pattern and edge callout because the lining rolled outward. We kept the opening target and logged the buyer's later coverage request separately. That kept the reason for each revision clear.

We quote before sampling. A quote can work from visible assumptions. Sampling needs agreed instructions, and bulk release needs accepted fit, materials, grading and packing. Before you export, cross-check the component references, drawing callouts and measurement rows. Date the PDF, keep its editable source, record the approval and note which version it replaces.

In our beginner trial, a founder who could sketch but had no patternmaking training spent 4 hours preparing this brief. They spent another 2 hours fixing undefined measurement endpoints and conflicting lining notes. The founder paid our USD 50 per-design fee, which included the tech pack and CAD pattern. Physical samples and courier delivery cost extra. The timed work covered preparing and correcting the document, not developing the sample.

Use that split to plan your own effort. You define the look and coverage, then bring in technical help for patterns, measurement methods and material trials. Keep in mind that the trial's timing applies only to that founder and that brief.

What does a clothing tech pack look like?

A clothing tech pack is a set of annotated garment drawings, materials and measurement tables, construction details and revision pages. You can expect front and back flat drawings, plus close-ups of details such as lining attachments. See Shopify's How To Create a Tech Pack To Send To Manufacturers.

Read across the pages and follow each component from its drawing callout to the bill of materials (BOM), the assembly instruction and the sample check. Here's that route through our scoop-back pack:

Page or viewEntry in our packCheck against the sample
Front and back flatsSHELL-NAVY identifies the exterior; back outline shows fuller seat coverageCompare neckline, scoop and seat with the drawings
Inside view and BOMLINING-SOFT matches the approved lining swatch; ELASTIC-LEG identifies the enclosed elasticTurn the suit inside out to inspect the lining and opening
Construction and measurement sheetTurn and secure the lining edge; check the finished opening along the marked relaxed measurement pathCheck the edge treatment separately from the opening measurement

The inside view shows the lining attachment, so you can compare it with the construction callout.

Remember the document map from the start: the measurement sheet sits inside the full pack. It doesn't supply the materials, construction and revision information around it, as Techpacker's What Is a Tech Pack? The Complete Fashion Guide explains. Include the separate cutting pattern in the handover. A sketch shaped like a pattern only explains the pieces, unless it has been developed and verified for cutting.

Try this review exercise, which uses assumed entries for the same scoop-back style. Read the original entries before you check the answer key. The dimensions here are exercise inputs, not sizing rules.

Original entriesFindingCorrected entry and approval before sampling
Inside drawing: LINING-SOFT; BOM: firm liningContradictionUse LINING-SOFT on both pages; brand approves the matching swatch
Strap detail: 10 mm elastic; BOM: 15 mm elasticContradictionUse 10 mm elastic in both entries; technician confirms construction and brand approves strap appearance
Measurement row: "leg opening," with no methodUnanswered decisionMeasure the full finished opening along its edge, relaxed, from the side seam back to that seam; technician marks the path and brand approves the method

To fix a contradiction, you have to remove the obsolete instruction everywhere it shows up. To fix an unanswered decision, you have to add an agreed instruction. Making the page look tidier fixes neither.
